Walsh-Hadamard Transform and p-Adic (Dis)continuity in Collatz Dynamics
Chen Mao
Contact: savy.ch@gmail.com, 1003838980@qq.com
Abstract: We introduce the Walsh-Hadamard Transform (WHT) into Collatz research, establishing it as the character group of 2-adic integers modulo 2K. We derive exact closed forms for the ramp signal, the folding formula, and the piecewise-constant support theorem. Concurrently, we refute the 3-adic continuity of σ via hierarchical mean tests and quantify a striking cross-character asymmetry: the 3-adic lattice energy is three orders of magnitude lower than its 2-adic counterpart, providing rigorous criteria for selecting appropriate harmonic analysis tools.

1. WHT Closed Forms and 2-Adic Characters (S-I1~I4)

Theorem 5 (WHT Closed Forms) For the ramp signal σ(2k)=k (N=2K), the WHT support is exactly K+1 lines:

W[q] = N(N+1)/2 (q=0),
W[2b] = −N·2b−1 (1 ≤ b ≤ K−1), and 0 otherwise.

Folding formula: W(σ)[q] = W(e)[q≫1] ± W(o)[q≫1], exactly separating the odd half (3-expansion domain) from the even half (dyadic domain). The odd half carries 55.67% of the total energy in the Walsh basis — the Walsh-domain quantification of the 3-expansion difficulty.

2. Refutation of 3-Adic Continuity (S-J2)

If σ were 3-adically continuous (class means converging mod 3j), it would imply spectral regularity. Our tests reveal:

  • The range of class means mod 3j grows with j (slope +0.886, γ=−0.886 < 0);
  • The argmax class drifts as [2,0,9,9,20] for mod 3/9/27/81/243, without a fixed 3-adic limit;
  • The range at 35 (9.16) is ≈3.5× the sampling noise.

Conclusion: Ramanujan-type 3-adic expandability fails; 3-adic harmonic analysis is unsuitable for decomposing σ.

3. Cross-Basis Character Spectrum Asymmetry (S-L5)

On the group Z/39 (N=19683), the 3-adic lattice (bins that are multiples of N/3) contains only 3.0e−5 of the total energy — comparable to Gaussian noise (3.9e−5). In contrast, the analogous 2-adic lattice on a 2-adic window holds 0.033, a three-order-of-magnitude difference. This quantitatively proves that σ's spectral structure is purely 2-adic; the 3-adic character basis reveals no point structure.
